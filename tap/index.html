CHAPTER ONE: FIVE DIMENSIONS, ONE MAXIMUM

The fifth dimension holds the most room a sphere ever will.

Give a ball of radius 1 more dimensions, one, two, up past ten, and its volume grows, then shrinks, forever after. The peak sits at exactly five, out of every dimension there is. Here is the formula behind that peak, built from scratch, animated, and explained.

scroll
01

What "volume" means past three dimensions

A disk is the set of points within radius r of a center, in a flat plane. A ball is the same idea in space: every point within r of a center. Nothing stops the definition there. An n-ball is every point within r of a center in n-dimensional space, and it has a well-defined n-dimensional volume, even though nobody can picture five directions at once.

The general formula for that volume is one of the more elegant results in geometry. It holds for every whole number n, and it is built from two familiar ingredients: π and the Gamma function, the smooth curve that extends factorials to non-whole numbers.

Vn(r) =  πn/2Γ(n/2 + 1)  · rn
the volume of an n-dimensional ball of radius r

Check it against the shapes everyone knows. At n = 2, Γ(2) = 1, and the formula collapses to π·r², the area of a circle. At n = 3, Γ(2.5) = 3⁄4√π, and it reduces to 4⁄3π·r³, the familiar volume of a sphere. The same rule that gives a circle its area gives a five-dimensional ball its volume. It simply keeps going.

02

Solving it for n = 5

Set n = 5 and the Gamma function needs one property: Γ(x + 1) = x·Γ(x). Unwinding it from Γ(7/2) down to Γ(1/2) = √π turns an abstract function into a plain fraction.

  1. Start with the general formula at n = 5.

    V5(r) = π5/2 / Γ(7/2) · r5

  2. Unwind Γ(7/2) using Γ(x+1) = x·Γ(x), three steps down to Γ(1/2).

    Γ(7/2) = 5⁄2 · 3⁄2 · 1⁄2 · √π = 15⁄8√π

  3. Substitute back in. The √π in the numerator and denominator cancel, leaving whole numbers and π².

    V5(r) = π5/2 / (15⁄8√π) · r5 = 8⁄15π² · r5
V5(r) = 8π²15 · r5  ≈ 5.2638 · r5
closed form, five dimensions

Differentiating with respect to r gives the four-dimensional "surface" that encloses this ball, the boundary of the five-dimensional shape: S4(r) = 8⁄3π²·r⁴, about 26.319·r⁴. Both quantities grow with the fifth and fourth powers of the radius, which is why the interactive curve below climbs so fast.

04

Why five, and not some other number

Fix the radius at 1 and instead vary the dimension. Two competing forces are at work in πn/2 / Γ(n/2+1): the numerator grows like an exponential, the denominator grows like a factorial. Exponentials start fast; factorials start slow but eventually overtake anything. Their tug of war produces a single hump, and it peaks at n = 5.

volume of a unit n-ball, by dimension
n = 5: the largest unit ball there will ever be, in any dimension

Push past five and the factorial in the denominator wins for good. By n = 10 a unit ball holds less volume than a unit ball in ordinary three-dimensional space. By n = 20 it is smaller than a grain of the volume it started with. Extra dimensions eventually cost more room than they add.

CHAPTER TWO

Entanglement's shortcut through time

Two particles, prepared together and then separated by any distance, stay correlated in a way ordinary space and time can't easily explain. One idea for why reshapes time itself into something like an hourglass: a single point where two distant places meet.

05

What entanglement actually does, and doesn't, do

Prepare two particles together so their properties are linked, then send them to opposite ends of the galaxy. Measure one and it lands on a result at random, spin up or spin down, there's no telling in advance. Measure the other, and it always comes out opposite. That agreement holds no matter how far apart the two are, and it shows up faster than light could have crossed the distance between them.

It's tempting to call that a message. It isn't. Each measurement is pure randomness, nobody gets to choose which result they see, so there's nothing to encode into it. The two people holding the particles only learn they were correlated once they compare notes afterward, over an ordinary, light-speed-limited phone call. Physicists call this the no-communication theorem: entanglement produces agreement, not a signal. It's been tested directly, with the two measurements timed so precisely that any ordinary signal would have needed to travel thousands of times faster than light to connect them, and still no usable information crossed the gap.

Every spin lands on a new, unpredictable angle, watch it change trial to trial. But A and B always land opposite each other, and always at the same instant. That's the whole phenomenon: agreement without a message.
THE PART THAT HOLDS UP

Correlation without communication. The outcomes agree; no message travels between them. Every serious model of entanglement, including the speculative ones below, has to preserve this or it contradicts a century of experiments.

06

One explanation: a wormhole in disguise

In 2013, Juan Maldacena and Leonard Susskind proposed a striking conjecture, now known by its shorthand, ER = EPR. It says that two maximally entangled particles, or entangled black holes, are connected by a wormhole: a bridge through spacetime joining two distant points. Picture two funnels, mouths flared open at either end, throats narrowing until they meet at a single point in the middle, an hourglass on its side. That's close to how physicists actually draw it.

the ER = EPR picture, not to scale
Both dots brighten together, not one after the other. The conjecture explains the correlation as geometry, a shared connection, not a signal sent from one mouth to the other.

Even inside this picture, the wormhole is not traversable. Nothing, not light, not a message, not a particle, can cross from one mouth to the other. The geometry ties the two particles' fates together without ever letting one side send the other side anything. ER = EPR is still a conjecture, actively studied and not yet confirmed, but it's taken seriously precisely because it doesn't break the no-communication rule above. It reframes entanglement as a shortcut in geometry, not a loophole in causality.

07

Could an extra dimension make the delay zero

Here's the more speculative layer, closer to what circulates in pop-science retellings of this idea. A handful of frontier physics papers explore models with an extra dimension, sometimes described as a hidden or "dark" layer of spacetime, where the usual speed limit doesn't apply. If information could move arbitrarily fast through that extra dimension, the proper time separating two distant points, measured through that layer, could shrink toward zero. Seen from ordinary three-dimensional space, two entangled particles a galaxy apart would look like they're sitting right next to each other through the extra dimension, even while looking impossibly far apart through the three we can walk around in.

A toy curve, gap = 1 / s, looping on its own. As the hypothetical extra-dimensional speed limit s climbs, the modeled delay between two entangled points falls toward zero, never quite a real measurement, only the shape of the claim.

This is genuinely close to the "time as a funnel" image: two points that are far apart in the dimensions we experience, but collapsed to almost nothing in a dimension we don't. It is not, however, settled physics. These are exploratory models, not confirmed by experiment, and they don't pin the effect to any one specific dimension, "the seventh" or otherwise; that detail isn't something the literature agrees on. What every version of the idea insists on is the same rule from earlier in this chapter: even with a shortcut through extra geometry, nobody gets to send a usable message faster than light. The shortcut explains the correlation. It still doesn't hand anyone a phone line.

WHERE THIS STANDS

Entanglement and the no-communication theorem: established, tested repeatedly. ER = EPR: a serious, unconfirmed conjecture under active research. An extra dimension collapsing time to zero: a speculative extension of that idea, worth thinking about, not something to treat as textbook fact yet.

Vn(r) = πn/2 / Γ(n/2+1) · rn, peak at n = 5, unit radius. Entanglement correlates instantly; it still can't send a message.
